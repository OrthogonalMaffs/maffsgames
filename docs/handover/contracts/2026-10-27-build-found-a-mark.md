TASK: Build "I Found a Mark": a method-only game in which students break the multi-mark questions they usually leave blank into steps, choosing the calculation for each step and banking method marks, never giving the final answer. First build: four question types, from a template bank the game imports. Unlisted.

ROOT CAUSE: New build for the priority audience (Jon, 27 Oct 2026). 81% of OCR Foundation marks sit in question parts worth 2+ marks (18 papers, 860 parts), where writing the right calculation earns method marks even if the arithmetic or the answer is wrong. Resit students leave these questions blank and score 0. Jon: "the marker WANTS you to get marks"; the feeling is finding a pound down the back of the sofa.

CLASS CHECK: Not a bug. Bank-first (canon §8.2.1): the question templates live in their own module, which this game imports and the 6 P's could later import; never copied. Every step is computed by the template from the question's numbers, correct by construction, and checked by a verifier through a separate code path.

EXACT CHANGE:
1. Template module (new file, e.g. schools/assets/found-a-mark-templates.js): each template generates a question (stem, optional diagram, mark value), its creditable steps, the dependencies between steps, and per-step distractors. A step is a calculation shown as text/KaTeX in terms of the question's numbers or a named earlier result ("the total"); the student never evaluates it.
2. The four types (Jon's rulings, 27 Oct), each 4 marks = 3 method + 1 answer (the answer mark is never asked for):
   (a) "Is it enough?": e.g. Sam has £50; 3 T-shirts at £8.50, 2 pairs of socks at £4.75; 20% off the total; enough? Steps: 3 × 8.50; 2 × 4.75 (any order); the total = their sum (after both); the discount, the total × 0.2, counts as a mark on its own; the discounted price, the total × 0.8 (or the total − the discount). Distractors: 3 + 8.50; 8.50 ÷ 3; 4.75 × 3 (wrong quantity); the costs multiplied together; the total − 20 (£20, not 20%). Constraints: 2–3 items, quantities 2–5, prices to 5p in £1–£20, discount 10/20/25%, budget within ±15% of the discounted total, either side.
   (b) Ratio with a twist: e.g. shared 3 : 5; Beth gets £60 more than Amir; how much shared? Steps: 5 − 3 (difference in parts); 60 ÷ (5 − 3) (one part); one part × (3 + 5). Distractors: 5 + 3 as the difference; 60 ÷ 8 (shares the difference as the total); 60 ÷ 3; 60 ÷ 5; one part × 5 (Beth's share, not the total); 60 × 8. "3 + 5" alone is not a step. Constraints: a < b coprime, a + b ≤ 12, whole-pound parts; contexts money, sweets, stickers, points.
   (c) Compound interest (calculator): e.g. £2,000 at 3% compound for 4 years. Steps: the multiplier 1.03 (or 103%); 2000 × 1.03 (or 2000 × 0.03, the first year's interest); 2000 × 1.03⁴ (or × 1.03 four times). Distractors: 1.3; 0.03 as the multiplier; 2000 × 3; 2000 × 0.03 × 4 (simple interest); 2000 × 1.03 × 4; 2000 × 1.3⁴. Variants: depreciation (multiplier below 1, distractor above 1); about 1 in 4 questions asks for SIMPLE interest explicitly, where 2000 × 0.03 × 4 is right and the compound calculation is the distractor (Jon: students must read which is asked). Constraints: £500–£20,000 in hundreds; interest 1–6%, depreciation 5–25%, whole numbers; 2–5 years.
   (d) L-shaped garden: a diagram with four labelled sides (e.g. bottom 10 m, left 8 m, top 6 m, lower right 5 m), turf at £6.50 per m²; cost? Steps: a missing length (10 − 6 or 8 − 5); the area of one part (6 × 8, 4 × 5, or 10 × 8); the whole area ((6 × 8) + (4 × 5), or (10 × 8) − (4 × 3)); the whole area × 6.50. Accept every valid route. Distractors: adding sides for a gap; adding all the sides (perimeter); 6 × 5 (sides of different parts); 10 × 8 alone as the whole; the area ÷ 6.50; the perimeter × 6.50. Constraints: whole-metre sides 3–15 m, cut-out at least 2 m each way, price £2–£12 to 50p; diagram labels consistent; any text in the diagram at least 12 px in every mode.
3. Play: the whole question with its mark value; "What could you write down?"; the student taps a calculation from the valid steps currently unlocked plus distractors; any order among unlocked steps. Right: "Oooh, a mark." and a coin appears in a "marks found" row. Wrong: name the misconception in one line, no penalty. "Show me a step" reveals one, tagged "with help", not counted in the score or on the leaderboard. When all method steps are found, or the student chooses "Bank it and move on": "You found 3 method marks. The last mark is for the answer, and you've already done the hard part." Session: 5 questions; headline = unaided method marks.
4. Look: an adult living room, warm and understated, not a cartoon; coins as the picture, the score in marks; a small clink, sound off by default; reduced motion respected. Theme row by the lowest tier served (GCSE Foundation). Copy as above; Jon adds any jokes later.
5. Game page in games/i-found-a-mark/, using theme.css and the shared components as they are; KaTeX for expressions; matching by dataset.val; the phone-fit rules in canon §7.6 (options, feedback and Next above the fold at 320 × 568, 375 × 667 and 390 × 844, and at 1366 × 768 and 1280 × 720); analytics with existing events only; leaderboard for unaided method marks per session, by existing board conventions (if a board needs a shared-file change, leave it off and report). Unlisted.

Verifier (new file): for many seeds per type, recompute every step's value independently, check every step is derivable from the question, every distractor differs from every valid step's value, both L-shape routes give the same area, and diagram labels are consistent. Plants, each caught: a distractor equal to a valid step; a missing dependency (a step offered before its inputs); a hinted mark counted in the score.
