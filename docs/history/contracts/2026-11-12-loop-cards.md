TASK: A generator that builds double-sided, guillotine-ready loop-card packs ("I have … / Who has …?") from a game's own question bank, each pack holding three separate decks (16, 24 and 32 cards), and two packs: Six Sevens, Bruv (free) and Like Terms Collector (paid, £3 on Tes).

ROOT CAUSE: The planned printables page needs a "Printable games" section with one free and one paid resource (Jon, 12 Nov 2026). No tool makes printable card games; hand-made decks would not be checked against the games' banks, and a loop with a duplicate or missing answer silently breaks in a lesson ("none get left out", Jon). Removing cards from a loop breaks it, so smaller classes need their own complete decks.

CLASS CHECK: A new shared tool, not a bug fix. It must serve any game with a question bank, so game specifics live in one small adapter per game (question source, independent recomputation, typesetting, any special cards), never in the layout or loop code. Approval, DRAFT badge, --final, the offline browser and pinned fonts follow tools/pack-generator: import offline_context, wording_approved, draft_badge and check_final from build_pack.py, and match its page footer (page(), :680); never copy them.

EXACT CHANGE:
1. New tools/loop-cards/: build_deck.py, one adapter per game (six_sevens.py, like_terms.py), README.md, and decks/<slug>/config.json (title, seed, wording_approved, as the pack configs). Output to dist-packs/ only (gitignored); the build refuses to write anywhere git tracks, as build_pack.py does.
2. Three decks per pack: 16, 24 and 32 cards (2, 3, 4 sheets). Each is its own complete loop with its own deck code (SS-16, SS-24, SS-32; LT-16, LT-24, LT-32) printed on every card. Decks may share questions; within a deck everything below holds.
3. Card layout: A4 portrait, 2 columns x 4 rows, each card A7 (105 x 74.25 mm), edge to edge with no gutters, identical grid on every sheet, so a stack is cut with 4 guillotine cuts (1 vertical, 3 horizontal). Thin dashed cut lines on fronts only. Every text element inside an 8 mm safe zone from each card edge.
4. Front: small "I have", then the answer large. Back: small "Who has", then the question large, ending "?". Small on both sides, in one corner: deck code and a card ID. Card IDs are a shuffled numbering, never loop order. Small on both sides, in the opposite bottom corner: "maffsgames.co.uk", subtle (about 60% grey, still legible printed in black and white), 9 pt, inside the safe zone, never overlapping the question or answer.
5. The loop (per deck): card k's back asks the question whose answer is on card k+1's front; the last card's back asks card 1's answer. One closed cycle through every card of that deck. Sheet positions shuffled by the seed so sheet neighbours are not loop neighbours.
6. Duplex page order per deck: front 1, back 1, front 2, back 2, … Backs mirrored for flip on long edge: front (row r, column c) has its back at (row r, column 1−c).
7. Single-sided fallback per deck, after the duplex sheets: front | back side by side (210 x 74.25 mm) with a fold line, 4 per A4, same safe zone, the web address on both halves.
8. Teacher pages: SHORT, scannable, no paragraphs. The build fails if page 1 has more than 120 words outside its table, any step is more than 15 words, or any text block runs past 3 lines. Use exactly this structure and wording (Project Claude's draft; Jon approves via wording_approved; change nothing without his word):
   PAGE 1, title "<Game> loop cards: how to run it"
   - Numbered steps:
     1. Pick your deck from the table. Print it.
     2. Hand out every card. Some students get two.
     3. Anyone starts: read your back aloud.
     4. Got that answer on your front? Say "I have…", turn over, read your back.
     5. It ends when the first card's answer comes back round.
   - Table "Which deck?": up to 16 students → 16 deck · 17–24 → 24 deck · 25–32 → 32 deck · more than 32 → work in pairs, one card per pair.
   - Two one-line tips: "Pairs work for any class: nobody stands alone with a wrong answer." / "Group race: print one deck per group. First table to close the loop wins."
   - Never remove cards: one line, "Use every card in the deck. Missing cards break the loop."
   - Six Sevens pack only, one line: "Two cards are SHOUTED. Let them."
   - Boxed "Printing" (max 3 lines): "Double-sided · flip on long edge · actual size (100%)." / "Print one sheet first; hold it to the light to check." / "No double-sided printer? Use the fold-over pages (p. N)."
   PAGE 2 onward, "Answer sheets": one table per deck, loop order, columns Card ID | I have | Who has. Nothing else on these pages.
9. Adapters and content:
   six_sevens: facts a x b with 2 <= a <= b <= 12, excluding x10; per deck choose cards with all products distinct, deterministic from the seed, weighted to the 6, 7, 8, 9 and 12 tables. EVERY Six Sevens deck (16, 24, 32) contains the fact 6 × 7, whose answer 42 is unique in the deck. Questions "a × b" (U+00D7), either order, except the shout question below. Products recomputed independently in the check.
   THE SHOUT PAIR, in every Six Sevens deck:
   - The ASKING card: the back that asks for 42 reads, in capitals, "WHO HAS SIX SEVENS?" (in words, no "6 × 7"), with a small "SHOUT IT" above it.
   - The ANSWERING card: the front whose answer is 42 shows "I have 42" as usual, and under it, larger than the answer and unmissable in black and white (bold, boxed or inverted): "SHOUT: I'VE GOT SIX SEVENS, BRUV!" The number 42 stays on the card so the holder must still know 6 × 7 = 42.
   - No other card in any pack has a shout or words-for-numbers. The answer sheet marks both rows "(shout)".
   - The check recomputes "SIX SEVENS" as 6 × 7 = 42 and requires it to match exactly the answering card.
   like_terms: the 30 STAGE3 items (games/like-terms-collector/index.html:290 onward) plus 2 STAGE2 items (:277 onward) in expression form (e.g. "3x + 2x + 4y + y" → "5x + 5y"), all answers distinct; the 16 and 24 decks are subsets. Variables italic, true minus (U+2212). Every answer recomputed from its expression by an independent parser (coefficients per letter) and compared as coefficient maps, never strings.
