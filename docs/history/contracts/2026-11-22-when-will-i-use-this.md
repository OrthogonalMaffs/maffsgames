THE 14 SCENARIOS (approved 10 Nov 2026, verbatim):

Topic: Ratio. One-line answer: "Whenever things have to be mixed in the right amounts."
1. Bricklayer · Construction and the built environment. Concrete for a shed base is often mixed 1 : 2 : 3: 1 part cement, 2 parts sand, 3 parts gravel, in buckets of the same size. You need 0.3 m³ of dry mix. Steps: Total parts: 1 + 2 + 3 = 6. | One part: 0.3 ÷ 6 = 0.05 m³. | Cement 0.05 m³, sand 0.10 m³, gravel 0.15 m³. Source: Jewson, "How to make concrete".
2. Hairdresser · Hair and beauty. Colour is mixed with developer in the ratio the maker gives. Wella's Koleston Perfect is mixed 1 : 1, but its Special Blonde shades are mixed 1 : 2. You use 40 g of a Special Blonde shade. Steps: Colour : developer = 1 : 2. | Developer = 40 × 2 = 80 g. Source: Wella Professionals, Koleston Perfect usage booklet.
3. Hospital nurse · Health and science. Liquid medicine comes at a strength, such as 250 mg in 5 ml. Nurses use: volume = what you want ÷ what you have × what it is in. The prescription is 125 mg. The bottle says 250 mg in 5 ml. Steps: 125 ÷ 250 = 0.5. | 0.5 × 5 = 2.5 ml. Source: Royal College of Nursing, "Safety in numbers: dosage for liquid medicines".

Topic: Percentages. One-line answer: "Every time a price, a strength or a limit is 'per hundred'."
4. Plumber · Construction and the built environment. A VAT-registered plumber adds 20% VAT to the bill. To add 20%, multiply by 1.2. The job costs £350 before VAT. Steps: £350 × 1.2 = £420. | The VAT is £70. Source: GOV.UK, "VAT rates" (rate checked 10 Nov 2026).
5. Electrician · Construction and the built environment. Voltage drops along a long cable. The wiring rules (BS 7671) allow at most 3% on a lighting circuit and 5% on others. The UK supply is 230 V. Steps: Most allowed on lighting: 3% of 230 = 0.03 × 230 = 6.9 V. | A lighting circuit drops 5.4 V. As a percentage: 5.4 ÷ 230 × 100 = 2.3%. | 2.3% is under 3%, so it passes. Sources: NICEIC, "Calculating voltage drop" (Professional Electrician), citing BS 7671 Appendix 4; Skills England, Installation and maintenance electrician (OCC0152), route Construction and the built environment.
6. Baker · Catering and hospitality. Bakers write recipes in "baker's percentages": the flour is always 100%, and everything else is a percentage of the flour's weight. A dough is 70% water and 2% salt. You use 500 g of flour. Steps: Water: 70% of 500 = 0.7 × 500 = 350 g. | Salt: 2% of 500 = 0.02 × 500 = 10 g. Source: King Arthur Baking, "Baker's percentage".
7. Jeweller · Creative and design. Gold is measured in carats out of 24. 9 carat gold is 9 parts gold in 24. The UK hallmark writes it as 375: 375 parts gold in every 1000. Steps: 9 ÷ 24 = 0.375, which is 37.5%. | A 9 carat ring weighs 4 g. Gold in it: 4 × 0.375 = 1.5 g. Source: Assay Office London, hallmarking guidance.

Topic: Area and perimeter. One-line answer: "Whenever you cover, paint, plant or mark out a space."
8. Painter and decorator · Construction and the built environment. A room is 4 m by 3.5 m, with walls 2.5 m high. There is a 2 m² door and a 3.5 m² window. Two coats; the tin says 1 litre covers 16 m² (example figure). Steps: Perimeter: 4 + 3.5 + 4 + 3.5 = 15 m. | Wall area: 15 × 2.5 = 37.5 m². | Take away door and window: 37.5 − 5.5 = 32 m². | Two coats: 64 m². Paint: 64 ÷ 16 = 4 litres. Source: Dulux, "How much paint do you need?".
9. Groundsperson · Agriculture, environmental and animal care (sports turf). Groundspeople mark out pitches. The FA's recommended adult pitch is 100 m long and 64 m wide. Steps: Lines round the edge (perimeter): 2 × (100 + 64) = 328 m of white line. | Grass to cut (area): 100 × 64 = 6400 m². Sources: The FA, "Guide to pitch and goalpost dimensions"; Skills England, Sports turf operative standard (ST0210), which lists marking out playing surfaces.
10. Farmer · Agriculture, environmental and animal care. Fertiliser is spread at a rate in kg per hectare. 1 hectare = 10 000 m². A field is 250 m by 120 m; the plan is 150 kg per hectare (example figure). Steps: Area: 250 × 120 = 30 000 m² = 3 ha. | Fertiliser: 3 × 150 = 450 kg. Source: AHDB, Nutrient Management Guide (RB209), which gives every rate in kg/ha.

Topic: Averages and statistics. One-line answer: "Whenever one reading isn't enough to trust."
11. Practice nurse · Health and science. One blood pressure reading can mislead, so NICE says to take readings at home, twice a day for 4 to 7 days, ignore the first day, and use the mean of the rest. After day 1, the top (systolic) numbers are 138, 134, 136, 140, 132, 136 (example figures). Steps: Add them: 816. | Mean: 816 ÷ 6 = 136. | The doctor compares that average, with the clinic reading, against NICE's limits. Source: NICE guideline NG136, Hypertension in adults.
12. Health visitor · Health and science. Babies' weights are plotted on growth charts with centile lines. A centile shows how many healthy children are expected to be below that line. A baby is on the 91st centile. Steps: 91 out of 100 healthy babies the same age are expected to weigh less. | 100 − 91 = 9 out of 100 are expected to weigh more. Source: Royal College of Paediatrics and Child Health, UK-WHO growth charts.
13. Ecologist · Agriculture, environmental and animal care. You can't count every wild orchid in a meadow, so ecologists count them in a few 1 m² squares (quadrats) placed at random, and scale up. Ten quadrats count 3, 5, 2, 4, 6, 3, 4, 5, 2, 6 (example figures). The meadow is 1500 m². Steps: Total: 40. Mean: 40 ÷ 10 = 4 orchids per m². | Estimate: 4 × 1500 = 6000 orchids. Sources: Oak National Academy, "Estimating population size using quadrats"; Skills England occupational map (Ecologist).
14. Meteorologist · route null. "Warmer than average" means compared with a 30-year average. The Met Office now uses 1991–2020. Steps: UK mean temperature, 1961–1990: 8.3 °C. 1991–2020: 9.1 °C. | Change: 9.1 − 8.3 = 0.8 °C warmer. | How a mean works: five Julys of 19.2, 20.1, 18.7, 21.3 and 19.7 °C (example figures) total 99.0; mean 99.0 ÷ 5 = 19.8 °C. Source: Met Office, climate averages notes and "How have daily temperatures shifted in the UK's changing climate?".
