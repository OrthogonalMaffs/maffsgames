ID:          patrol-lcm-timer
LEVEL:       KS3 / GCSE
TOPIC:       Number / Lowest common multiple
INSTRUMENT:  Digital countdown timer, 1 to 200 minutes, whole minutes.
CONTEXT:     Three head prefects patrol the corridors around the control room at different speeds. The only safe moment is the one where all three are back in the staffroom at once.
CLUE:        "Patrol rota, from 12:00. A: a lap every 12 minutes. B: every 15. C: every 18. Set the timer to the first minute after 12:00 when the corridor is empty."
ANSWER:      180
AHA:         All three are back together only at a time that is a whole number of laps for each of them - the lowest common multiple, not the sum and not the product.
MISCONCEPTION: 45 (adding 12, 15 and 18 as though the laps stack).
SOLVE:       12 = 2^2 x 3, 15 = 3 x 5, 18 = 2 x 3^2.
             LCM = 2^2 x 3^2 x 5.
             = 180 minutes.
TIME:        4
VERIFY:      [m for m in range(1, 201) if m % 12 == 0 and m % 15 == 0 and m % 18 == 0]

ID:          cube-surface-volume
LEVEL:       KS3 / GCSE
TOPIC:       Geometry / Surface area and volume
INSTRUMENT:  Four-digit mechanical door keypad, 0000-9999.
CONTEXT:     The control room door is locked, and the Head of Estates has left a note on the keypad for whoever keeps resetting it.
CLUE:        "The code is the coolant tank's capacity in cubic centimetres. I am not telling you that. The tank is a perfect cube and its total surface area is 1350 square centimetres. Work."
ANSWER:      3375
AHA:         Surface area goes back to a length through a square root and forward to a volume through a cube - six faces first, then one face, then one edge, then the cube of it.
MISCONCEPTION: 225 (dividing 1350 by 6 and entering the area of one face).
SOLVE:       One face = 1350 / 6 = 225 square centimetres.
             Edge = root 225 = 15 cm.
             Volume = 15^3 = 3375 cubic centimetres.
TIME:        3
VERIFY:      [v for v in range(10000) for x in range(1, 50) if x**3 == v and 6 * x**2 == 1350]

ID:          canteen-freezer-inequality
LEVEL:       GCSE
TOPIC:       Algebra / Inequalities, with a prime constraint
INSTRUMENT:  Wire frequency tuner, 0 to 50 Hz, whole Hz.
CONTEXT:     The override panel is a nest of coloured wires, each humming at its own frequency. The manual is specific, and the failsafe reroutes the canteen freezers.
CLUE:        "Isolate the wire whose frequency f is prime and satisfies 20 < 3f - 5 < 32. Any other wire cuts the freezers."
ANSWER:      11
AHA:         Solving the double inequality leaves four whole frequencies, so the inequality alone does not identify a wire - the prime condition is doing the second half of the work.
MISCONCEPTION: 12 (solving to f < 12.33 and taking the largest whole number in range, ignoring "prime").
SOLVE:       20 < 3f - 5 < 32.
             25 < 3f < 37.
             8.33 < f < 12.33, so f is 9, 10, 11 or 12.
             Only 11 is prime.
TIME:        4
VERIFY:      [f for f in range(51) if 20 < 3*f - 5 < 32 and f > 1 and all(f % d for d in range(2, f))]
