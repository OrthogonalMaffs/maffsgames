It's Just Base 60, Bruv

Time counts in 60s, not 100s. Hours, minutes, clocks and what the calculator really means.
Why 60?Why 60?

An hour is 60 minutes, not 100. Blame the Babylonians, who got it from the Sumerians, about 4,000 years ago. Nobody knows for certain why they counted in 60s. The best guesses:

  • 60 shares nicely. It has 12 factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30 and 60. 100 has only 9. 60 is the smallest number that 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 all go into, so halves, thirds, quarters, fifths and sixths of an hour are all whole minutes.
  • Count it on your hands. Your thumb can count the 12 finger segments on one hand; the five fingers of the other hand keep track of the dozens. 12 × 5 = 60.
  • The circle. A full turn is 360 degrees, which is 6 × 60, and close to the number of days in a year. 360 has 24 factors.

Your calculator counts in tens. Your clock doesn't. It's just base 60, bruv.
