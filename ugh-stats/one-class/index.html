Case 3 · outliers · mean v median

One class, 3,667 answers

1Ask

On 12 November I looked at the last four weeks: 13,664 answers in 28 days, a mean of 488 a day. I was pleased. That looked like growth. Was it?

2Collect

Then I looked at the days. On Tuesday 3 November a single class in Malaysia answered 3,667 questions. On 23 October a class in Wales answered 3,099. Take those two days out and the other 26 days average 265. Two classes, on two days, had nearly doubled my "typical" day.

4Decide

A value that sits far away from the rest is an outlier. The mean adds every value, so one outlier drags it a long way. The median only looks at the middle value, so it barely notices. This is why the Office for National Statistics reports the median wage as its headline figure for pay. A few very high earners pull the mean up, so the mean describes nobody's pay packet. The median is what the person in the middle earns. So when we talk about a typical day now, we use the median, and we report big class days on their own.

What this data can't tell us

  • We can't be certain a big day is one class: we see a burst of answers from one country in a short time, not who sent them.
  • Answers aren't students.
  • Our own testing is in the numbers.
